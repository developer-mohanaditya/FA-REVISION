## Per-Module Content Plan

### Module 1 — Introduction to Financial Accounting & The Accounting Equation

**Source:** Session 1 slides, pp. 1–28  
**Exercise link:** Verdi Case 1

**Learning outcomes (from slides):**
1. Explain what financial accounting is and who uses it.
2. Define the accounting equation (Assets = Equity + Liabilities).
3. Classify a transaction as affecting assets, equity, or liabilities.
4. Apply the equation to 4 transaction types (capital, asset purchase, loan, sale).
5. Distinguish profit (economic) from cash (bank balance).

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Four Verdi-style transactions (capital contribution, loan, purchase, sale) traced through the accounting equation with a before/after balance-sheet table.
- Clean numbers (100, 50, 30, etc.) aligned with Verdi case logic.

**Quiz distribution (12 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: definition of equity, classifying assets, impact of transactions on equation, cash vs profit distinction, users of accounting
- 2 T/F: "Bank balance always equals profit" (F), "Equity = share capital only" (F)
- 2 Short answer: what does the accounting equation represent? What is enrichment?
- 2 Calculation: given transactions, show before/after BS; calculate profit from revenue/expenses
- 1 Spot the error: student journal that violates equation balance

**Question styles derived from:** Exam Training T/F questions 5, 6, 10; Final Quiz Part 1 Q1

### Module 2 — Financial Statements & Accounting Principles

**Source:** Session 1 slides, pp. 29–43  
**Exercise link:** Verdi Case 2 (prepare financial statements)

**Learning outcomes:**
1. Describe the purpose and structure of the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement.
2. Calculate net income from the income statement (Revenue – Expenses).
3. Identify which financial statement each accounting item appears on.
4. State the core accounting principles (entity, periodicity, going concern, prudence/caution, historical cost).
5. Describe the role of the annex.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Simple income statement: three revenue lines, four expense lines → profit calculation.
- Balance sheet classification exercise: given 10 items, classify as asset, liability, or equity.

**Quiz distribution (12 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: which statement shows economic performance, definition of equity, what the annex does, balance sheet date meaning, going concern definition
- 2 T/F: "Net profit appears in both the balance sheet and the income statement" (T), "Equity = total liabilities" (F)
- 2 Short answer: explain the difference between the income statement and the cash flow statement; define historical cost
- 2 Calculation/classification: classify 8 items into correct financial statements; compute net profit from given expenses and revenues
- 1 Spot the error: a balance sheet where total assets ≠ total liabilities + equity

**Question styles derived from:** Final Quiz Part 1 Q1–Q4; Exam Training T/F Q5, Q6

### Module 3 — The Double-Entry System & the Accounting Process

**Source:** Session 2+3 slides, pp. 1–22  
**Exercise link:** Verdi Case 2 (journal → ledger → trial balance)

**Learning outcomes:**
1. Apply debit/credit rules to all account categories (assets, liabilities, equity, expenses, revenues).
2. Record transactions in a journal with correct accounts and amounts.
3. Post journal entries to T-account ledgers.
4. Prepare a trial balance and verify that debits equal credits.
5. State the accounting process flow (documents → journal → ledger → trial balance → statements).
6. Name the core accounting principles relevant to recognition timing.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Capital contribution + asset purchase: two-entry sequence showing journal → ledger → trial balance.
- Asset vs expense distinction applied to two similar purchases (computer vs office supplies).

**Quiz distribution (13 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: which side of the T-account increases an asset, which account is credited when a sale is made on credit, trial balance purpose, asset/expense distinction, when to recognise revenue
- 2 T/F: "Debit always means increase" (F — depends on account type), "Total debits must equal total credits in the trial balance" (T)
- 3 Short answer: describe the accounting process; explain why double entry is required; define the going concern principle
- 2 Journal entry: record equipment purchase for cash; record credit sale
- 1 Spot the error: trial balance that does not balance

**Question styles derived from:** Exam Training exercises 2, 3, 4; Final Quiz Part 1 Q3, Q4

### Module 4 — Daily Operations: VAT & Payroll

**Source:** Session 2+3 slides, pp. 23–44  
**Exercise link:** Windsurf S3

**Learning outcomes:**
1. Explain how VAT works as a tax neutral to company performance.
2. Record a sales invoice (ex-VAT revenue + VAT collected + gross receivable).
3. Record a purchase invoice (ex-VAT expense + deductible VAT + gross payable).
4. Compute and record VAT payable / VAT credit.
5. Describe the payroll structure: gross, employee contributions, net salary, employer contributions.
6. Record two payroll journal entries (recognise, then pay).

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Complete VAT cycle: sales invoice, purchase invoice, VAT settlement, payment.
- Payroll example: gross 100, employee share 20, employer share 40, net pay 80.

**Quiz distribution (13 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: VAT is paid by whom (final consumer), which account receives VAT on sales (VAT collected = liability), VAT credit meaning, normal balance of "VAT collected" account, employer vs employee contribution classification
- 2 T/F: "VAT collected is an income for the company" (F), "Net salary = gross salary" (F)
- 2 Short answer: explain why VAT is neutral for company performance; describe the 2-entry payroll sequence
- 3 Journal entry: record sales invoice with VAT; record purchase invoice with VAT; record payroll recognition
- 1 Spot the error: VAT entry where ex-VAT amounts and VAT amounts are confused

**Question styles derived from:** Exam Training exercises 6, 7; Final Quiz Part 1 Q5, Q6, Q7; Exam Training T/F Q8 (dividend-related)

### Module 5 — Fixed Assets: Depreciation, Disposal & Impairment

**Source:** Session 4 slides (all 39 pp.)  
**Exercise link:** Windsurf S4

**Learning outcomes:**
1. State the criteria for recognising an asset as a fixed asset (PP&E).
2. Apply straight-line and accelerated (diminishing balance) depreciation.
3. Calculate net book value (NBV) at any given date.
4. Record a disposal in 3 steps: catch-up depreciation, derecognise, record proceeds.
5. Apply the impairment test and record an impairment loss.
6. Distinguish depreciation from impairment.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Straight-line depreciation schedule: 4-year life, pro-rata entry.
- Disposal: acquisition cost 6,000; accumulated depreciation 4,000; sale proceeds 2,500 → journal entries.
- Impairment: NBV 18,350; recoverable amount 15,000 → impairment loss 3,350.

**Quiz distribution (14 questions):**
- 6 MCQ: PP&E recognition criteria, which method gives higher early depreciation (accelerated), NBV calculation, what triggers an impairment test, difference between depreciation and impairment, gain/loss on disposal (proceeds > NBV → income)
- 2 T/F: "Depreciation affects cash flow" (F — non-cash), "Impairment can be reversed" (T)
- 2 Short answer: list 3 impairment indicators; explain the 3-step disposal procedure
- 3 Calculation/journal entry: compute depreciation year 2 of accelerated method; prepare NBV at disposal date; record the full disposal entry
- 1 Spot the error: disposal entry where accumulated depreciation is forgotten

**Question styles derived from:** Final Quiz Part 1 Q8; Exam Training exercise 2; Windsurf S4 case

### Module 6 — End-of-Period Entries: Adjustments, Inventory & Provisions

**Source:** Session 5 (all 25 pp.) + Session 6 (all 24 pp.)  
**Exercise links:** Windsurf S5, Windsurf S6 (adjusting entries portion)

**Learning outcomes:**
1. Identify and record the 4 types of adjusting entries (accrued expenses, accrued income, prepaid expenses, deferred income).
2. Apply the periodic inventory system to record opening/closing inventory changes.
3. Distinguish the direction of inventory change for purchased vs manufactured goods.
4. Recognise and record impairment of receivables, inventory, and marketable securities.
5. Apply the 3 criteria for recognising a provision; record provision and reversal.
6. State the financial-statement impact of each adjusting and end-of-period entry.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Prepaid insurance: 12-month premium paid mid-year; compute year-N and year-N+1 portions.
- Accrued electricity expense: estimate for Dec; invoice arrives in January.
- Receivable impairment: customer in difficulty, 40% recoverable → record impairment (ex-VAT base).
- Provision for employee dispute: record and partial reversal.

**Quiz distribution (15 questions):**
- 6 MCQ: what type of adjustment applies to a rent invoice covering next year, direction of finished goods inventory change (CI-BI = revenue side), VAT in impaired receivable (not impaired — recoverable from state), provision criteria (3 cumulative), which account is debited for accrued expense, reversal of provision (income or expense?)
- 2 T/F: "Impairment of inventory is a cash expense" (F), "A contingent liability must be provisioned" (F — only if probable)
- 3 Short answer: explain the 4 adjusting entry types; describe what "accrued income" means; list 3 examples of provisions
- 3 Journal entry: record prepaid rent; record inventory change; record impairment of receivable
- 1 Spot the error: student impairs a receivable using the VAT-inclusive amount (should use ex-VAT)

**Question styles derived from:** Exam Training exercises 1, 5; Final Quiz Part 2 (Rex: insurance, payroll); Windsurf S5, S6 cases

### Module 7 — Income Tax & Profit Distribution

**Source:** Session 6 Part 2 slides (all 16 pp.)  
**Exercise link:** Windsurf S6 (income tax + net income portion)

**Learning outcomes:**
1. Explain the difference between taxable income and accounting profit.
2. Calculate corporate income tax at 25%.
3. Record the income tax expense entry (year N) and identify when it is paid (year N+1).
4. Compute net profit after tax.
5. Calculate distributable profit and record profit appropriation (reserves and dividends).
6. Record the payment of dividends and its effect on financial statements.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- CIT calculation: profit before tax 380,000; taxable income 400,000 (non-deductible fines added back); CIT = 100,000; net profit = 280,000.
- Profit distribution: net income 200,000; 60% retained earnings, 40% dividends; record distribution entry in year N+1 and payment.

**Quiz distribution (12 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: CIT rate in France, what account records CIT expense (69), when CIT is paid (year N+1), what "reserves" are (not cash), definition of distributable profit
- 2 T/F: "Reserves can be used to pay a supplier" (F), "Profit distribution has no impact on the income statement" (T)
- 2 Short answer: explain why taxable income may differ from accounting profit; explain what happens at the General Meeting regarding profit allocation
- 2 Calculation/journal entry: compute net income given profit before tax + taxable income; record the profit distribution entry
- 1 Spot the error: student records CIT as an asset rather than a liability

**Question styles derived from:** Exam Training T/F Q8, Q10; Final Quiz Part 1 Q2; Windsurf S6 case

### Module 8 — The Cash Flow Statement

**Source:** Session 7 slides (all 11 pp.)  
**Exercise link:** Windsurf S7

**Learning outcomes:**
1. State the 3 sections of the cash flow statement and what each measures.
2. Distinguish items that affect profit but not cash (non-cash) from items that affect cash but not profit.
3. Compute operating, investing, and financing cash flows from a list of transactions.
4. Reconcile the change in cash with opening and closing cash balances.
5. Verify consistency: CFS closing cash = balance sheet closing cash.
6. Classify any given transaction as operating, investing, or financing.

**Worked micro-examples planned:**
- Four transactions: (a) depreciation – non-cash, profit impact only; (b) sale of equipment – investing cash; (c) new loan – financing cash; (d) collections from customers – operating cash.
- Simple CFS from Windsurf S7 opening balance sheet + selected transactions.

**Quiz distribution (12 questions):**
- 5 MCQ: depreciation in the CFS (operating — added back / or non-cash, not in direct method), dividend payment in CFS (financing), where does an equipment purchase go (investing), what qualifies as cash equivalent (≤3 months, highly liquid), which CFS section reflects company operational performance
- 2 T/F: "A profitable company always has positive operating cash flow" (F), "Depreciation reduces cash" (F — non-cash)
- 2 Short answer: explain the difference between profit and cash; explain why operating cash flow is important for financial analysis
- 2 Calculation: given a list of 8 transactions, classify each and compute the 3 CFS subtotals
- 1 Spot the error: CFS where a loan repayment is included in operating cash flow

**Question styles derived from:** Final Quiz Part 1 Q3; Exam Training T/F Q7; Windsurf S7; Final Quiz Part 3 (Beethoven CFS)

## Exam Format Analysis & Question Style Decisions

### Actual exam format (from final quiz 2025-2026):
- **Part 1:** 10 single-choice MCQ (no penalty for wrong answers) — 5 marks
- **Part 2:** Case with ~4–6 journal entries (some complex: insurance with prepaid, payroll, etc.) — 10 marks
- **Part 3:** Full forecast financial statements (15 transactions × double-entry + 3 statements) — 15 marks

### Adaptation decisions for in-app quiz (documented):
1. The actual exam uses a table-based journal entry format (account number / wording / debit / credit). The app will replicate this structure visually in the "reveal solution" view.
2. Part 3 (full forecast statements) is too long for a per-module quiz item. Instead, M8 includes a "How to prepare forecast statements" worked example, and the Exam Mode page provides a practise CFS checklist.
3. Spot-the-error questions are not in the actual exam format but are educationally valuable for beginners and are clearly labelled as such.
4. T/F with rationale questions are drawn from the exam training format (T/F/Don't know).
5. Short-answer questions are answered with "Compare with expected answer" reveal, not auto-graded.

## Safe Assumptions (To Be Confirmed)

1. **LIFO exclusion:** LIFO is mentioned in session 5 slides as a valuation method option. The course examples only use FIFO and WAC. App treats LIFO as "mentioned but not used in French GAAP practice" and adds a note.
2. **VAT rate:** App uses 20% standard rate throughout (matches all exercises).
3. **CIT rate:** App uses 25% (matches session 6b slides).
4. **Direct method CFS:** Only method shown in slides. App does not introduce the indirect method.
5. **Accounting standards:** App uses French GAAP (PCG) as primary framework throughout, consistent with all slides.
6. **Pro-rata depreciation:** All depreciation examples in slides include pro-rata temporis (days/12 months). App applies this consistently.
7. **Windsurf S4 accelerated depreciation:** The slide example uses a "double the straight-line rate" logic consistent with French accelerated depreciation. The app follows this without introducing MACRS or other foreign systems.
